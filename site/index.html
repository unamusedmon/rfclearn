Science-backed study plan

Study like a hunter, not a hostage.

You do not need to absorb 228 RFCs in one tragic caffeine opera. The research-backed move is smaller: pick a mission, read one meaningful chunk, try to recall it from memory, and let spaced review do the heavy lifting while your ego takes a brief but educational hit.

Daily loop

The 25-minute orbit

  1. 2 minutes: Pick one track and skim the hunting context so your brain knows why this RFC matters.
  2. 12 minutes: Read one RFC or one major section. Stop before fatigue turns prose into wallpaper.
  3. 4 minutes: Close the page and write or say three things you remember. No peeking. Mild annoyance means it is working.
  4. 5 minutes: Run Study Due for low-stakes retrieval with feedback.
  5. 2 minutes: Leave one note: weird field, likely detection clue, or one thing Future You will forget on purpose.
Weekly rhythm

How to not bounce off the material

  • Four focused sessions: Stay inside one sprint so the protocol family starts to cohere.
  • One mixed session: Use the protocol map plus due cards to interleave concepts and dependencies.
  • One light day: Catch up, reread a threat indicator, or take a guilt-free rest day. Sustainability beats martyrdom.

After the six core sprints, use the update-chain tag as your side-quest generator.

Implementation intention

Make Future You dramatically less slippery

If-then plans help turn vague good intentions into visible action. Keep the cue specific and the task tiny enough that your internal gremlin has a weaker legal case.

If your cue happens, then your study move lives here. Tiny counts. Tiny is how empires are built.

Why this routine works

  • Spacing beats cramming for long-term retentionCepeda et al. (2006) · Distributed practice reliably improves later memory compared with massed review, so the plan uses short repeat visits instead of one heroic weekend binge.
  • Retrieval practice outperforms passive restudyAdesope et al. (2017) · Low-stakes recall and quizzing help learning stick better than rereading, which is why each session ends with a brief memory dump and due-card review.
  • Classroom quizzing boosts learning, especially with feedbackYang et al. (2021) · Practice tests improve achievement across levels and formats, and feedback makes the payoff bigger, so the site leans on repeated review rather than one-and-done reading.
  • Motivation improves when autonomy and relevance are supportedWang et al. (2024) · Learners persist more when they have choice, a manageable path, and a clear reason to care. That is why the library now offers tracks instead of a giant undifferentiated RFC buffet.
  • Interest-triggering reading interventions work bestde Nooijer et al. (2024) · Reading motivation improves most when content feels interesting and meaningful, so each sprint is framed like a hunting mission rather than mandatory vegetables.
  • If-then plans make starting more likelyGollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) · Specific implementation intentions help translate good intentions into action, so the homepage includes a tiny cue-and-response pact you can save locally.
